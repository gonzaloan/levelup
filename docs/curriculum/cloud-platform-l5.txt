CURRICULUM — cloud-platform-l5 (Cloud & Platform, L5)

Written 2026-07-27 after walking concept 1 with the owner. This is the contract the
six concepts are written against, so the writing is judged against a stated intent
rather than against taste.

WHO ARRIVES HERE
Someone who can already operate one service (L4). At L5 they set the cloud approach
for a TEAM'S systems — so the question stops being "does this work" and becomes
"what breaks together, and what am I willing to pay to separate it".

THE ONE QUESTION THE MODULE TRAINS
  What breaks together, and what does it cost to separate?
Every concept is one answer. Every answer is a PURCHASE: it has a price, and there
is a cheaper option that has to be ruled out first.

WHAT THE LEARNER CAN DO AT THE END
Given any isolation proposal, say four things out loud:
  1. What the mechanism IS, in one sentence a peer would recognise.
  2. What it BUYS — stated as a bound, not as "more reliable".
  3. What it COSTS — capacity, latency, operational surface, or reversibility.
  4. What would have to be TRUE for the cheaper option to win.

WHY THE FRAMING IS "ISOLATION", NOT "CLOUD SERVICES"
A catalogue of managed services teaches recall. "What breaks together" is a question
the learner can re-ask on their own system tomorrow, which is the only thing that
transfers. Concepts 2-6 are resilience patterns that happen to be bought from a
provider — that's fine: the provider is where the price tag is.

ORDER, AND WHY IT CHANGED
Old: cells -> shuffle sharding -> static stability -> multi-region -> data platform
     -> account boundary.
The ACCOUNT BOUNDARY MOVES FROM LAST TO FIRST. It is the cheapest and strongest
isolation available, and it was arriving after the learner had already been sold
cells. Teaching the expensive mechanism first and the cheap one last inverts the
exact judgment this module claims to train. Everything else keeps its order: bound
the blast radius of TENANTS -> of INFRASTRUCTURE -> of DECISIONS.

WRITING RULES FOR EVERY CONCEPT IN THIS MODULE
Each rule exists because the old version of concept 1 broke it.

1. OPEN WITH THE DEFINITION. The first thing on the pane says what this IS, in
   plain words. The old opener — "Trains the judgment of deciding whether bounded
   per-cell impact is worth the routing layer" — presumes the concept inside the
   sentence meant to introduce it.
2. BULLETS AND BOLD, NOT PROSE. Bold carries what must be retained; a reader
   scanning only the bolded words should get the shape of the concept.
3. PLAIN LANGUAGE. "Bounded per-cell impact" -> "a failure only reaches that cell's
   customers". Jargon is allowed only after it has been defined on the page.
4. BUYS / COSTS / CHEAPER-FIRST, EXPLICITLY. These three are the module's spine, so
   they get their own labelled section instead of being buried in a paragraph.
5. ANALOGY LAST. It settles a concept the reader already has; as an opener it is a
   metaphor for something they haven't met.
6. CODE ONLY WHERE IT TEACHES. Measured on the current artifacts, the share of
   comment-only lines is the tell:
       Cells (TypeScript, 33 lines)      45%  CUT   — a router implementation does not explain what a cell is
       Shuffle sharding (Python, 19)     21%  KEEP  — the collision calculation IS the concept
       Static stability (HCL, 30)        53%  CUT   — two ASG blocks differing by a number is a caption, not code
       Multi-region                       —   already a widget
       Data platform (SQL, 29)           69%  KEEP, TRIMMED — one dataset read by two engines is the point
       Account boundary (JSON, 34)       68%  KEEP, TRIMMED — the IAM policy is what shows WHY tags don't suffice
   Where code is cut the concept keeps its diagram; nothing loses its visual.
7. A WORKED EXAMPLE STAYS on every concept. That is where the numbers live, and
   tools/check-trace.cjs requires any figure in an artifact to trace back to it.

THE SECTION TEMPLATE (from the owner-approved rewrite of concept 1)
  1. WHAT IT IS          definition, plain, 2 short paragraphs max
  2. WHAT YOU BUY / DON'T BUY / PAY   three bullets, bolded labels
  3. WHEN IT MAKES SENSE  the trigger, plus the cheap alternatives to try first
  4. WHAT IT REQUIRES     the non-negotiables — without these it isn't the thing
  5. ANALOGY              one, at the end

## What shipped

The Codex's 11 clusters each gained a `primer` — the level ABOVE their entries.

The defect it fixes was not in any entry. All 107 passed every gate: six-part anatomy
complete, `cost` as a bound, `cheaperFirst` named. What was missing sat above them:
every cluster oriented the reader with ONE line of tagline and then handed over up to 18
sibling techniques as a flat list; 49 of 107 entries were DAG roots so they all arrived
as peers; and the umbrella term was never defined — "RAG" appeared 126 times in
`codex.json` while "retrieval-augmented generation" appeared **0 times** there and 0 in
`lessons.json`.

Each primer is a general-to-specific descent: what the umbrella term IS, why it exists
(with a figure an entry below actually states), the AXIS every member varies along, the
FAMILIES as a total partition, and the ordered questions that land on one entry.
11 primers, 36 families, 107 of 107 entries grouped. Nothing folds.

## Open, deliberately not fixed here

`rag-failure-taxonomy` enumerates seven RAG failure points and the evaluation cluster
ships six entries — FP7 "incomplete" has none. Both reviewers independently called this a
CONTENT gap rather than a primer defect: the family rule says "each of these", not "the
seven", so nothing claimed is false, and weakening the primer would hide the hole instead
of closing it. Recorded as a ratchet in `tests/codex-primer.test.ts` (`KNOWN_MISSING = 1`)
that fails if the gap GROWS and turns green when `rag-incomplete-answer` is authored.
